]]> Comment on Reclaiming Our Digital Selves: HCCF’s Vision for a Human-Centered Top-Level Domain by CMay https://hccf.onmy.cloud/2026/06/21/reclaiming-our-digital-selves-hccfs-vision-for-a-human-centered-top-level-domain/#comment-57 Wed, 01 Jul 2026 01:54:01 +0000 https://hccf.onmy.cloud/?p=1026#comment-57 People are so distracted talking about the choice of PDF. It’s fine. The problem is that the document is not interesting, the HCCF website is not a good demonstration that the organization understands what people-centric means as you have to struggle to even find any real person named on their social media or website. It seems to use soulless stock photos and lacks self-expression, which does not walk what it talks.

That is all in addition to the general lack of detail about how the finances are supposed to work. A lot of companies have excess capital to offer public goods and many times those offerings come with many challenges that you need to have the resources to face, which many companies do.

How are you going to moderate the TLD to make sure that every subdomain adheres to the right principles and spirit of the free service? How are you going to solve the trust issues of people needing to provide both their full name, address and their always-current IP address consistently to a non-profit with limited resources and questionable security practices compared to a company like Google or Cloudflare who have the funds to hire outstanding security researchers with active persistent investment to maintain that posture?

This is a problem I’ve spent some time thinking about and do have my own thoughts on solutions, but in some ways the feeling we are trying to solve comes from a filtering issue. One problem is that, there are actually more personal websites than ever, but over the decades search engines have deprioritized them in search results not exclusively as a result of companies vying for spots, but because of stalking and other forms of abuse. The reduction in the personal feeling of the web is coming from multiple directions and some of the logic behind that change is reasonable.

You can make a new Angelfire or Geocities, but Github and Cloudflare are already similar aside from the missing directory component. Web directories used to be everywhere and you could volunteer into a directory. Online phone books also used to be a charming part of the web. Now phone books and web directories are mostly not gracing people’s screens anymore.

Even with a directory and solving the hosting, you still face the e-mail hosting problem, where major e-mail providers like gmail already solve so many critical issues around e-mail. Then, if a user is hosting personal information on their site and has their full name in their e-mail address, for many people it will become an e-mail address they don’t want to use on every website as it will simply make it easier for companies they don’t particularly trust to profile them.

I say this because, .self might have a spirit of personal technological sovereignty, but handling the VPN and e-mail already makes that largely performative while posing as many risks as they solve. So the larger perceived mission is to make the web feel more personal, which requires actually creating connections since there is no lack of personal websites in practice, only connections.

There are TLDs of all kinds and I have my doubts that simply a well intended philosophy will be enough without addressing the angles the problem stems from to start with. It’s more of a verification, technology, security and abuse problem than a lack of will for people to express digitally in a personal way.

We need to rethink home internet routers, processor security, search engines, browsers, smartphones and verification.

Cloudflare solved many issues already from a personal or security perspective, but not in a way that increases the connectivity between people. I’m not opposed to HCCF making an attempt, but even with help from for-profit companies (which could be an inevitable requirement) a transformative impact is doubtful without a holistic approach.

Unless you reframe your goal to simply make it easier for people to self-host their websites and ditch all of the larger vision. That’s been done with some success and if it could inspire more people to learn about hosting or how the internet works, then it might do some good in other ways.
